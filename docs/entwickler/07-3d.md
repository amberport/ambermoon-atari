# 7. 3D

## Who does what

| Who | What |
|---|---|
| Thalion's `MAP/3D_MAP.S` | the scene: walls, objects, sky, horizon, light level |
| Thalion's rasterisers from `AM2_CPU`: `put_dpoly` (walls), `draw_zoomshape` (objects), `Insert_horizon`; `Draw_floor`/`Draw_ceiling` (textured floor and ceiling, 68020 code) | draw into Thalion's **3D buffer**, never onto the screen. Extracted at build time (appendix C) |
| Thalion's `Fade_colour`, also from `AM2_CPU` | not a rasteriser: mixes the colours for the light level (`Set_D3_light_level`) |
| `D3_SUP.S` (ours) | `Show_MOVEP_screen`: the buffer onto the screen, with bank planes and gradients; `Clear_1plane_block`; `D3_einrichten` |
| `COMBAT_SUP.S` (ours) | the combat zoom: the blitter part of `Draw_zoom_shape`/`Draw_zoom_silhouette` as CPU code. Thalion's preamble and `Zoom_tabellen` come from `AM2_CPU` |

Thalion's rasterisers are pure memory code, which is why we take
Thalion's machine code instead of rewriting it (decision of 2026-09-28;
how to go back if that turns out wrong: `docs/02-3D-Renderpfad.md`, A7).
They save no registers at all, `a5` and `a6` included; their callers in
Thalion's code know that, and ours must too.

## The 3D buffer

144×144 pixels, 4 planes in **MOVEP format**: every 8 pixels take 4
bytes, one per plane, so 16 colours. The stars lie on plane 4 and are
drawn directly onto the screen; `Clear_1plane_block` clears that plane in
every frame.

`Show_MOVEP_screen` writes the buffer with `movep.l` into our 8-plane
screen. The 3D view uses the second palette of its raster list, so bank
0, and planes 5–7 stay zero. In lines of a gradient (`R_range`, the
sky of 3D cities) it asks `Zeilenersatz` (`RASTER.S`) for the line's
gradient colour and slot, and every pixel of the gradient colour gets the
slot: planes 0–3 and 5–7 from the slot, plane 4 untouched. That covers
the sky, the sky-coloured points below the horizon and the floor texture
in colour 9, as the copper does on the Amiga.

## Floors and ceilings, CPU and caches

Whether textured floors and ceilings are drawn is decided by Thalion's
code through `CPU_68020_plus`. `D3_einrichten` sets that flag from the
cookie `_CPU` (`D3_CPU` = AUTO) and switches the 68030 caches on; the
program's end restores the old cache register (`Cache030_zurueck`), the
emergency exit too.

**Speed** (findings 201, 202): in a dungeon the Falcon needs 5 VBL per
frame without floor and ceiling and 9 with them, so Thalion's
`Draw_floor`/`Draw_ceiling` cost about 4 VBL. Our side is exhausted;
more needs routines of our own for floor and ceiling. The user accepted
that on 2026-10-09, as a deviation for playability; it is not built yet,
and before it is, the way, the expected gain and a picture check against
Thalion's version go to the user.

## On the ST

`D3_SUP.S` branches under `IFD ZIEL_ST` to `St_movep_schirm` in
`ST_LIB.S`, which puts the buffer onto four planes through the colour
table; three places in `COMBAT_SUP.S` do the same for the combat zoom.
No floors and ceilings (68000).

**Recoloured copies** (user, 2026-10-10; finding 242, docs/12): when Thalion claims the
`MOVEP_handle` for a frame, `St_abschrift_bild` makes sure that the wall,
object and horizon blocks exist as copies whose bytes are already register
numbers, in one `Malloc` block outside the handle table. While the frame
is active, `Claim_pointer` returns these copies instead of the originals,
and `Sky_colour`/`Floor_colour` hold registers too; so Thalion's rasteriser
writes registers into the buffer, and `Show_MOVEP_screen` only copies it
with `movep` (`St_abschrift_zeigen`). The copies are recoloured when the
colour table changes (not for light levels, which keep the table), and are
dropped when one of their handles is freed or invalidated
(`Free_memory`, `Invalidate_memory`, `Kill_unclaimed_memory`). Without
memory, with more than one table over the 3D lines or with floor and
ceiling, the frame takes the old way through `St_movep_schirm`.

A7: the extraction from `AM2_CPU`).
